Dermatology call guide

A step-by-step guide for handling inbound Dermatology calls. Scroll down the chart, answer each question, and open a section when a yellow box tells you to.

Five rules for every call

  1. Confirm who you are speaking with before you share anything: two identifiers, and check for release restrictions. No match, no PHI.
  2. Check for urgent items first.
  3. Follow decision tree 8083. If it says "Deny scheduling," do not book it.
  4. Send a TE for most requests. We no longer transfer calls to the offices, except for hotwords and hard stops. Use the pool number from the site grid, never a doctor's last name.
  5. Before you send a page or TE, check the spelling, the callback number and the GE number.

Three things that are different in Dermatology
  • Most requests are a TE, not a transfer. We no longer transfer calls to the offices, except for hotwords and the "Deny scheduling" hard stops.

Section 4: Scheduling skin appointments

Use this when a patient wants to schedule a new or established dermatology appointment. You will use decision tree 8083 (Book It, then the Dermatology tree).

Visit type, provider and booking

The tree fills in the visit type for you after you answer. Use the first yes below when the tree asks.

Registration text after booking

Insurance rules
  • Traditional Medicaid / Public Aid / HFS: Duly no longer participates. This now includes the old rule for patients seen twice in the last 2 years. Exceptions: a one-time hospital follow-up, or a global requirement.
  • BCBS Blue Community: only internal (Duly PCP). Use the Internal Medicaid visit type. The patient needs a referral from the PCP. No external plans.
  • Medicaid patients cannot be self-pay. Check OneSource (Passport) for Medicaid. If they have it, refer them back to their PCP.
  • No Duly PCP: do not book new specialty visits, unless it is a one-time hospital follow-up (then refer to their local PCP) or a global period requires the care.
  • Access DuPage: send a TE to the office site (all sites).
  • Medicare: an annual skin exam must be at least 1 year and 1 day after the last skin exam.
  • Referrals: required only for HMO plans. Always attach the referral to the appointment.
  • Fees: co-pays are now collected for routine full body exams. Self-pay consults start at $117 and established visits at $70, due at the time of service.

Other scheduling rules
  • Transfer of care: doctors prefer the same MD or PA, but you can book another provider when theirs is booked out. There is no transfer-of-care limit or rule.
  • Sooner appointment: offer a PA or another provider, and the wait list. If they decline both, see Section 6.
  • Provider comments show in 3 places on a provider's template. Read them before you book.
  • "Follow up with my specialist" appears only for established patients, only when the reason is General Derm, Spot Check or Hair Loss, and only when the patient meets the Epic rules.

Send the page in PerfectServe

Follow these steps every time you page. Never skip a field.

Before you click send: put the patient's GE number first in the Reason for Call, double-check spelling against Epic, and check the callback number.
Page the right person
  • Patient calls: type the provider the patient sees. Never override the call schedule; if someone else is covering, page them.
  • Do not page a specialty provider who has never seen the patient. Offer a new-patient visit. For a new-patient hotword, schedule a same-day or next-available visit.
  • You can page for hotwords when the hotword line is unavailable, urgent symptoms, and questions about a procedure the patient has the next day.
  • You do not page for routine results, appointment issues, billing, refills, non-urgent symptoms, forms, or medical advice for non-patients.
  • A patient who is in the hospital right now cannot page a doctor; they speak with hospital staff.

Tell the patient what to expect, and know what to do if the page is not answered.

  • Reason for page (medical concern or hotword)
  • Phone number called from
  • Time of page
  • Provider paged
  • Reason for the RL6 (for example unanswered, or multiple pages after 1 hour)
  • Established provider, if different from the provider paged
  • Whether a TE was sent to notify the provider

Section 9: Identity and privacy

Use this on every call before you share anything, and for any request that could change a patient's email, phone or login information.

High-risk requests: email, phone or login changes

Minimum necessary and good habits
  • Only look at what you need to do your job, and only share the minimum needed.
  • Never look up your own record, a family member's, a neighbor's or a coworker's unless it is job-related and leadership approved it.
  • Lock or log off your workstation when you step away. Never share or write down passwords.
  • Do not have patient conversations in public or common areas.
If you think PHI was shared by mistake
  • Tell leadership right away and email Compliance@duly.com. The compliance hotline is 630-545-7600.
  • Include the patient name, DOB, account or GE number, the date, how it happened, and any steps already taken.
  • Follow your department's protocol for noting the account.

Section 10: Language Line (interpreter)

Use this when the caller needs an interpreter. Use the transfer campaign "IB D Language Line" or "150 (Language Line)". The interpreter joins the call, so you can help the patient on the same call.

Interpreter calls cannot be transferred to another extension or phone number. If the caller needs to be transferred, the next agent must get another interpreter.
